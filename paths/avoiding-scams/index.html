Learning path

Avoiding Scams: The Defense Playbook

Recognizing scams before you sign anything. Covers drainer attacks, address poisoning, romance scams, fake support, and red flags that should stop you cold.

All holders 40 minutes 5 steps

Who this is for

Anyone who holds crypto or is about to. The attacks covered here have been used against people at every level — from first-time buyers to professional traders.

This path takes about 40 minutes. By the end you'll have a working mental model of how crypto scams actually work, the reflexes to recognize common patterns, and a habit (the wallet safety checklist) that dramatically reduces your exposure.

The single most important rule

No legitimate organization will ever ask for your seed phrase. Not for verification. Not for security. Not for an upgrade. Not for any reason. The moment someone asks for it, you are talking to a scammer.

If you remember nothing else from this path, remember that.

When to come back

Re-read the [Wallet Safety Checklist](/tools/wallet-safety-checklist/) before any non-routine transaction. Run it as a habit, not as a one-time thing. Most people who lose funds to drainer attacks say "I knew the rules, I just wasn't thinking in the moment." The checklist is the intervention that turns the rules into a reflex.

What if you've already been hit?

The articles in this path cover prevention. If you've already been scammed:

  1. Disconnect and assess. Move remaining funds to a fresh wallet if you can.
  2. Report to the FBI IC3 at ic3.gov — crypto theft is a federal crime.
  3. Report to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
  4. Do not engage with recovery services. Anyone promising to recover your funds is running a second scam.

Then come back to this path and read it carefully — the pattern you fell for is documented here, and now you'll recognize it.
